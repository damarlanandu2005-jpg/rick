## B. Findings that change the picture
1. **The Blender executor already exists.** `templates/blender_look/from_lock.py` (READ): builds the camera from `camera_baked.json` one key per frame (vertical fov, DOF focus on the subject's card), places one card plane per character with the drawing swapped per frame from `index.json`, imports the locked set `.glb` and swaps every `mat:<tag>` material for the library material of that name, computes `keep_clear` discs for dressing, and refuses to run if the plan changed after the lock (sha1). Our Pass A used only a fragment (`from_lock.card`). My spec's "new executor required" (section 10 item 1) was wrong.
2. **Attachment is solved by hand points, not new sockets.** `pieces/fx/fly_and_hit.js` (READ): "from = the muzzle point in a real hand, on the frame the hand fires; to = where it sticks"; a timetable gives leave and hit frames; the piece is "technique as numbers, no three.js", applied by the set module. `cards/meta.json` hand offsets exist. So "sockets" were my invention.
3. **Set authoring in Ricks is a small three.js module per place** (`sets/platform.js` is 50 lines for the demo) tagged with `mat:` names that Blender converts. Blender then adds materials, light, air and dressing (`scatter` with keep-clear). Our `kit/` (set recipe + scatter + density gate) therefore fits as the Blender dressing stage on top of a simple locked layout, not as a replacement for the layout stage.
4. **The cheap loop works here with no GPU**: planning (0.2 s), storyboard (19 s), visibility (1.5 s), frame checks (4 s), lock (31 s). Only the full animatic is slow here (about 1 s/frame).
5. **The checkers find real defects.** frame_checks caught a cut head in Ricks' own demo in 4 s. check_film disagrees with plan check on two numbers: the gates are not interchangeable and the example is not clean.
6. **Pitfall notes (4 read)** are concrete pre-flight tests that Ricks mostly already bakes into the plan or the checkers (e.g. keys mean *arrive* with `lead` 5 frames; horizontal fov in 9:16 is narrow, stage in depth; stale camera near/far; body inside furniture = `body_in_mesh` check).
7. **Blender 5.x compatibility (READ, not run):** `from_lock.py` already handles layered actions (4.4+/5.x) with a `fcurves` fallback and uses `surface_render_method` with a fallback. `cine.py` guards `action.fcurves` with `hasattr`, so on 5.x the interpolation-fix block is silently skipped (effect on easing unknown until run in Blender). `blender_look` ran on Colab 5.2.4 in our rounds (look, grade, lights, scatter, from_lock.card). `cine.py` has not run in our Blender.
8. **Effects**: `film.json` `fx` play as placeholder rings in three.js; the real effect is look-pass work in Blender from `FX.md` recipes. Blender effect recipes remain a genuine gap; timing numbers come from the pieces.

## F. Second reading pass (same day; READ unless marked)
1. **Material library is procedural.** `blender_look/materials.py` LIB holds about 30 shader-node recipes (fabric, canvas, burlap, wood_grain, plywood, bark, plaster, brick, roof_tile, painted/chipped metal, metal, brass, rubber, turf, wet mud, cobble, stone, foliage, blade, matte, water, straw, wool, rope, tar wood, boat paint, dirt) plus glass, emissive lamp, fire and cloud. "No downloaded textures" is the Ricks rule; image tiles enter only through the optional `image_name` for big painted surfaces. So `blender_look/tex` is optional for the library to run, and needed only for that painted-surface route (and its colour correction).
2. **Presets**: five light/sky presets (night, morning, day, golden, interior) in `look.py` and five matching grade presets in `grade.py` (gain, saturation, vignette, pull, tint, shadow desaturation). Light helpers: sky, sun, practical, air, mist, cloud, moon. `scatter`: blades, place, cluster3, field with exclude. These are the pieces our Western kit already used.
3. **A three.js set module is dense hand-authored code** (box/cylinder helpers; every mesh gets a name and a role or exact library `mat`; it returns `anchors` (named points such as `clock`, `train`) and an `update(f,t)` for secondary motion). The Ricks set-builder writes one of these per place; our `kit/` is the equivalent authoring effort in bpy. Named anchors exist for props, hand offsets for characters.
4. **Camera pieces** (`camera_pieces.json`) are film.json snippets for five patterns: push-in on a named thing, low-angle close-up on a mark, fly-through an object to change place, fly-land-rest (a stretch = one take of 6–17 stations), the "hundreds" count. No projectile-chase piece in this file.
5. **CINEMATOGRAPHY.md section 4 answers the duel/bullet question by name**: duel = `mix_path(A,B)` + orbit with roll on each hit + push on the deciding blow; thrown/launched = `ballistic_path`, catch from low, apex on the punch word, kick and dust on landing; projectile impact = `object_path` of the projectile, slow push, contact 0–3 f before the word, kick 7 f, flash ≤2 f, pull-out; POV (cockpit, saddle, gunsight) = `mount`. Binding rules: 3–4 shots per 30 s, 2–4 rig hand-offs inside a shot with 14–30 f blends, track about 30 % of frames, lead room sideways only (capped 0.35 × distance), stage in depth in 9:16.
6. **Ricks' own route for action shots** (section 5): the camera plan (paths + rigs) is written at shot-plan time; in the "full Blender world" order the action shots are blocked with the same rigs and shown as a quick low-res Blender animatic (25 % size, 8 samples, about 1 s per frame) before Stage 2. Proof shot: 7.3 s, EEVEE 64 samples, 1440×2560 about 3.5 s/frame on an RTX 2070 Super (preview 0.65 s). **Open question:** how a chase of a moving object is previewed in the three.js loop (film.json stations only?). Not yet verified.
7. **Pitfall library (all 27 titles read, 4 in full)**: mostly pre-flight facts that a plan or checker can test (z-fighting floors, one-frame light changes, 9:16 horizontal fov, key frames meaning arrive vs depart, body inside furniture, walks sliding on a linear move, dissolve frequency, density at half the reference, shade bands on faces, white-hot palettes bleaching saturation) plus process pitfalls (parallel renders killing batches, split folders). Seven solution notes (light-pool vignette, far ink fades with haze, `flicker.py`, locked look as a package, merge stretch folders, private render folder, lock file).
8. **SCORECARD**: 8 categories (colour day/golden/night, lighting, ink, textures, characters, depth, composition/camera) with numeric pass ranges measured on 20 reference Shorts. It still lists "ink on world 60 % of edges" — older than the newest "no ink on the world" rule: another internal contradiction to resolve, not decided.
